Practical guide

How to convert an Arabic PDF to Word and check the result

A useful conversion is more than a DOCX download: text should be editable and tables, numbers and headings should remain understandable. This guide helps you choose a mode before converting.

In brief: A PDF tournament bracket or branching diagram is not necessarily a Word table. PDFs often store visual positions rather than the original Word structure.

1. Identify the type of PDF

Open the file in a PDF viewer and try to select and copy a word into a text editor.

  • Selectable text: the PDF likely contains a text layer that a converter can extract.
  • Text cannot be selected: the page may be scanned or stored as an image and may require OCR.
  • Mixed document: some pages may contain text while others contain images, drawn tables or diagrams.

OCR recognizes text; it does not automatically recreate the original table or diagram layout.

2. Choose a conversion mode

Editable text extraction

Best for straightforward text documents. Text can be edited, but columns, tables, Arabic reading order and spacing may change.

Editable tables and layout (experimental)

PDF Globe attempts to build a Word table grid using text and line positions. It is more likely to work when rows and columns are clear and consistent. Complex diagrams can produce misplaced text or empty cells, so inspect every page before relying on the result.

Hybrid conversion

Ordinary text pages remain editable. A page detected as complex may be embedded as a full-page image to preserve its arrangement. Text on that image page is not editable.

Preserve original appearance

Every PDF page is placed in Word as an image. Use this when visual similarity matters more than editing words.

3. Tips for Arabic and right-to-left layouts

  • Check paragraph direction and table direction, not just individual words.
  • Review names, numbers and dates, especially where Arabic and English are mixed.
  • Check column order: visual order in a PDF may differ from the reading order inferred by a converter.
  • Uncommon or embedded fonts may be substituted when the DOCX is opened on another device.

Do not inspect only the first page. Check pages from the beginning, middle and end of the document.

4. How to inspect a table or tournament bracket

  1. Open the DOCX in Word or LibreOffice.
  2. Click a player-name cell. Check whether Word displays table-cell boundaries and selection handles.
  3. Temporarily edit a name, then undo the change.
  4. Check that match times remain associated with the correct match.
  5. Look for excessive empty cells or names placed in the wrong columns.
  6. Check bracket connectors; they may be separate drawings rather than part of a table cell.

Important: A branching tournament tree may not fit a regular row-and-column grid. Accurate reconstruction can require independent drawing objects or manual redesign rather than ordinary table conversion.

5. When not to rely on automatic conversion alone

Do not use the DOCX as a final copy without review when it contains schedules, scores, financial information or names that must remain attached to specific cells. Compare the result with the source and correct errors before printing or sharing.
